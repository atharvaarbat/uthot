Section 1 — The Problem (Gate: Is this real?)

Describe the problem in one sentence, without using the words "platform," "solution," "seamless," or "AI."
When did you last personally experience this problem? Write the date and what happened.
If you've never experienced it: who did, and how do you know? Name them.
What do people do today instead? Be specific — name the tool, spreadsheet, WhatsApp group, or intern.
Why is that workaround tolerable? People tolerate a lot. What makes this one intolerable?
On a scale of "annoying" to "I would pay today to make it stop" — where does this sit, and what evidence do you have?

The real question underneath: Is this a problem or a preference?
Section 2 — The Person (Gate: Why you?)

What do you know about this problem that most smart people don't?
Where did that knowledge come from? Years of work? A specific incident? Or an article you read?
What would a competitor have to do to acquire your knowledge? How long would it take them?
If you disappeared tomorrow, would this company still make sense?

The real question underneath: YC's "founder-market fit." Not passion — unfair advantage.
Section 3 — The Customer (Gate: Do they exist?)

Name three real people who have this problem. First and last name.
Have you spoken to them about it? What were their exact words? Not your summary — their words.
What surprised you in those conversations? If nothing surprised you, you weren't listening, you were pitching.
Who is the first customer? Not the market — the single first human who pays.
Where do they already gather? If you can't answer this, you have no distribution.
Who in the customer's organization loses if this succeeds? Every sale creates an enemy.

The real question underneath: a16z's "do you have earned insight or borrowed insight?"
Section 4 — The Falsification (Gate: Are you honest?)
This is the section most tools skip and the one that creates the most clarity.

Write the strongest argument against your own idea. Make it good enough that it scares you.
Why hasn't this been built already? "No one thought of it" is not an answer. Pick one: technology just changed, regulation just changed, a behavior just changed, or everyone who tried failed for a reason you understand.
Who tried this before and failed? Find them. What killed them? What's different now?
What has to be true for this to work? List the assumptions. Circle the one that, if false, kills everything.
What's the cheapest way to test that assumption this week? Not this quarter — this week.
If you ran that test and it failed, would you actually stop? Answer honestly.

The real question underneath: Thiel's "what important truth do very few people agree with you on?" — inverted into a survivability test.
Section 5 — The Wedge (Gate: Can you start?)

What's the smallest version of this that a real person would pay for?
Can you build that in 30 days? If not, cut it again.
What are you not building? Write the list.
How does someone hear about you the very first time? Trace the exact path.
What's your unfair distribution channel — the one competitors can't copy?

Section 6 — The Machine (Gate: Does it compound?)

Does this get better as more people use it, or just bigger? Explain the mechanism.
What stops a well-funded team from copying this in six months?
What does the business look like at $100M revenue? Work backwards: how many customers at what price?
Is that number of customers plausible? Show the arithmetic.

Section 7 — The Mirror (Gate: Do you actually want this?)

Why are you working on this instead of the other ideas you had this month? List the others.
Would you work on this for ten years if it never made you rich?
What are you avoiding by starting a company right now?
What would make you quit? Write it down now, so future-you can check.
